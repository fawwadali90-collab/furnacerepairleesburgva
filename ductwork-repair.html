Ductwork Repair

Where Ducts Fail in Loudoun County Homes

Attic ducts bake in summer and freeze in winter, working every joint and seam. Crawlspace ducts sit in dampness that rusts metal and feeds on flexible duct insulation. The original duct tape, despite the name, dries out and peels within years. We find the same failure points again and again because physics does not vary by neighborhood.

A pressure test quantifies the leakage instead of guessing. Numbers beat hunches when deciding how much sealing a system needs.

Sealing, Repair, or Replace: The Honest Ladder

Small leaks at joints and seams get sealed with mastic, the thick paste that actually lasts, not tape. Crushed or disconnected runs get repaired or replaced in sections. Ducts that are undersized for the furnace, common in homes with additions, may need resizing to fix chronic uneven heating.

We start with the least invasive fix that solves the measured problem, and say so plainly when a bigger step is warranted.

The Comfort Complaints Ducts Cause

One freezing bedroom, dust that never settles, a system that runs forever: ducts are behind more of these than furnaces are. A furnace can be perfectly healthy and still fail to heat a house whose ducts leak into the attic.

If your furnace is newer but comfort never improved, the ducts are the prime suspect. Mention the pattern when you call.

Common Ductwork Repair Questions, Answered

What Are the Signs of Bad Ductwork

Rooms that never match the thermostat, dust that accumulates no matter how often you clean, whistling or rattling at joints when the system runs, musty smells from the vents, and bills climbing while comfort drops. A duct pressure test confirms leakage with numbers instead of hunches.

Visual tells in attics and crawlspaces: disconnected runs, crushed flexible duct, old tape peeling away from seams, rust streaks on metal, insulation fallen away from the duct surface. Loudoun County attics are harsh on ducts, and the damage is usually visible once you look.

If the furnace is healthy but the house is not comfortable, the ducts are the prime suspect. Mention the pattern when you call (571) 561-3454; it shapes what the technician checks first.

Return duct problems deserve equal attention. Undersized or leaky returns starve the furnace of air, raising static pressure and tripping the limit switch, which homeowners experience as short cycling blamed on the furnace. Sealing and properly sizing the return side often fixes the mysterious cycling that no furnace part ever explained.

Can Leaky Ducts Make My Furnace Work Harder

Yes, measurably. Supply leaks dump heated air into attics and crawlspaces, so the furnace runs longer to satisfy the thermostat. Return leaks pull cold unconditioned air into the system, which the furnace must then heat. Either way, runtime climbs.

Longer runtimes wear everything faster: the igniter cycles more, the blower motor runs more hours, the heat exchanger endures more thermal cycles. A furnace connected to leaky ducts ages in dog years compared to one on tight ducts.

Sealing the measurable leaks is often the highest-return comfort improvement in an older home, and most sealing work finishes in a day. The furnace you already own performs like a better furnace once the ducts stop wasting its work.

Insulation on the ducts matters as much as sealing them. Bare metal ducts in an unconditioned attic lose heat through the duct walls even when every joint is tight, and sweating ducts in summer invite the moisture problems that rust them from the outside. Sealing plus proper insulation is the complete fix, and both are usually handled in the same visit.

How do I know if my ducts leak?

High bills, dusty rooms, uneven temperatures, and whistling at joints all suggest leakage. A pressure test gives the definitive answer.

Is duct sealing messy?

No. Most sealing happens in attics, crawlspaces, and at registers. Living spaces stay clean and the work is usually done in a day.

Can ducts be cleaned and sealed in one visit?

They are different services with different equipment. We can schedule both, but each gets its own proper visit.

How long do ducts last?

Metal ducts last decades; flexible ducts and insulation degrade faster, especially in harsh attic conditions. Twenty to twenty-five years is a typical planning horizon.

Will sealing ducts lower my bills?

Sealing significant leaks reduces the heated air escaping to unconditioned spaces, which usually shows up on winter bills.
