Gas vs Electric Furnace: What Loudoun County Homes Usually Have

Quick Answer

Most Loudoun County homes heat with natural gas where mains reach, propane in rural areas, or heat pumps with electric backup. Pure electric furnaces are less common. To identify yours, look at the fuel source: a gas meter or propane tank means gas, a large breaker and no fuel line means electric. Call (571) 561-3454 if you are unsure.

What Loudoun County Actually Uses

Leesburg and the eastern suburbs largely run on natural gas. Head west toward Purcellville, Hamilton, and Lovettsville and propane takes over. Heat pumps with electric backup strips are common in newer construction everywhere. Pure electric furnaces exist but are the minority.

How to Tell What You Have

Open the utility area and look. A gas furnace has a gas line, a vent pipe, and usually a visible burner compartment. An electric furnace has heavy electrical cabling, a large dedicated breaker, and heating elements instead of burners. A heat pump has an outdoor unit that runs in winter.

Why It Matters for Repairs

Gas and electric furnaces fail differently and need different parts and expertise. Gas work involves combustion safety. Electric work involves high-amperage circuits and sequencers. Knowing your system type helps you describe the problem and helps us arrive prepared.

Thinking of Switching

Fuel switching is a replacement-scale project, not a repair. If you are considering it, the decision involves utility availability, operating costs, and equipment compatibility. We discuss it honestly during replacement consultations. Call (571) 561-3454 to talk through your options.

Questions About Gas vs Electric Furnace

Which is cheaper to run in Loudoun County?

Natural gas usually costs less per unit of heat than electric resistance. Propane prices vary. Heat pumps are efficient in mild cold.

Can I convert from electric to gas?

If gas service reaches your home, conversion is possible as part of a planned replacement. It involves gas lines, venting, and permits.

Do heat pumps need a furnace too?

Many have electric backup heat for the coldest days. The backup strips are essentially a small electric furnace inside the air handler.
