Heat Exchanger Repair

Why a Crack Changes Everything

The heat exchanger is a metal chamber that gets blazing hot inside while your breathing air flows around the outside. A crack lets carbon monoxide and combustion gases mix into that air. It is the reason furnaces have rollout switches and why technicians treat exchanger findings as safety findings first and repair questions second.

Cracks usually start from years of overheating: restricted airflow, oversized furnaces, or neglected maintenance stressing the metal through thousands of expansion cycles.

How Cracks Are Actually Found

Some cracks are visible with a borescope camera snaked into the chamber. Others reveal themselves through combustion analysis: rising carbon monoxide in the flue, or flame disturbance when the blower kicks on. A technician uses both, because either test alone can miss what the other catches.

Be wary of any crack diagnosis made without a camera image or test numbers you can see. We show you what we find.

Repair vs Replace When the Exchanger Fails

Heat exchangers can sometimes be replaced under manufacturer warranty, but the labor is substantial and the rest of the furnace is still old. On a younger furnace with a warranty-covered part, replacement of the exchanger can make sense. On an older unit, a full furnace replacement is usually the sounder investment.

We lay out both paths with real numbers so the decision is clear.

Common Heat Exchanger Repair Questions, Answered

What Does a Cracked Heat Exchanger Smell Like

A cracked heat exchanger can push combustion odors into the supply air: a sharp metallic or chemical smell when the heat first kicks on, soot traces around registers, or an acrid odor that appears only while the blower runs. But many cracks produce no smell at all, which is exactly why smell is a poor safety test.

Carbon monoxide is odorless, so never rely on your nose. Any new unusual odor when the heat runs deserves a prompt inspection, and a sounding carbon monoxide detector means leaving the house and calling the fire department first.

Inspection with a borescope camera and combustion analysis finds what noses miss, and we show you the images and readings behind any finding. Call (571) 561-3454 for a heat exchanger inspection.

Rollout switches and flame sensors provide a layer of protection, shutting the furnace down when flame or heat goes where it should not. But these safeties are a last resort, not a monitoring system: they react to a failure already in progress. An inspection finds the crack before the safeties ever need to act, which is the entire point of looking before heating season.

Why Do Heat Exchangers Crack in Older Furnaces

Metal fatigue from thermal cycling. Every heating cycle expands the chamber when hot and contracts it when cool. Over 15 to 20 years and tens of thousands of cycles, stress concentrates at weak points until cracks form. It is wear, not defect.

Overheating accelerates the process. Restricted airflow from neglected air filters, undersized ductwork, or an oversized furnace makes each cycle hotter and the expansion more violent. A limit switch tripping repeatedly is the symptom; the heat exchanger absorbs the damage behind it.

Corrosion weakens the metal first in some cases: condensate issues or poor venting eat at spots that then crack under cycling. Annual maintenance catches the overheating pattern early, which is the main thing you can do to slow it down.

Oversizing is the overlooked accelerant. A furnace too large for the house satisfies the thermostat quickly and shuts off, then fires again minutes later, multiplying the thermal cycles the exchanger endures. Proper sizing at installation, verified by a load calculation rather than a guess at the old unit, is the long-term protection a heat exchanger needs most.

What are the warning signs of a cracked heat exchanger?

Soot around the furnace, a strong unusual smell when it runs, the carbon monoxide detector sounding, or visible cracks and rust on the exchanger itself.

Can a cracked heat exchanger be patched?

No. Patching is not a recognized repair. The safe options are exchanger replacement or furnace replacement.

How does a crack even happen?

Repeated overheating from poor airflow, an oversized furnace, or years without maintenance stresses the metal until it cracks.

Will my CO detector catch it?

Detectors are a critical backup, but they alarm at sustained levels. An inspection catches cracks before they become emergencies.

Is the furnace safe to run meanwhile?

If a crack is confirmed, the furnace should not run until repaired or replaced. We will make it safe on the spot.
